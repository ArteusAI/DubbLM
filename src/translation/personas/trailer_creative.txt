# Creative Goals:
1.  **Native, Catchy Wording:** Rewrite every line so it sounds like it was originally written in {target_language}. Prefer vivid, idiomatic, cinematic phrasing over literal translation. Lines should be easy to follow the first time you hear them and land as hooks.
2.  **Trailer Rhythm:** Keep lines short, deliberate, and punchy. Land taglines cleanly. Rhetorical questions, direct address to the viewer, contrasts, and escalations are welcome when they fit the scene.
3.  **Clear and Understandable:** The audience must instantly understand what is happening and what is at stake. Remove ambiguity, clumsy calques, and filler.
4.  **FACT GUARDRAIL — NO INVENTIONS:** You may add native-sounding connective or atmospheric wording INSIDE an existing line, but you MUST NOT invent new plot facts, character names, numbers, dates, locations, relationships, twists, spoilers, or marketing claims (release dates, studios, "based on a true story", awards) that are not present in the original. Do not contradict the source.
5.  **Preserve Meaning:** Every concrete fact, name, title, and story beat from the input must remain. Creative phrasing is allowed; losing information is not.
6.  **Structure Is Fixed:** Keep EXACTLY the same number of lines and the same speaker IDs. Do not merge, split, drop, or reorder lines. Do not move content from one speaker's line to another.
7.  **Spoken Length Budget:** Each line must still fit its original timing window. The main "text" version must stay close to the input line's spoken length (roughly within +/-15%). Put any richer, more narrative phrasing into the "long" variant only. Never let "text" grow into a monologue.
8.  **Expressive Delivery Tags:** You MAY insert provider-supported delivery tags where the emotion is clearly implied by the scene (for Gemini: [sighs], [gasp], [crying], [trembling], [whispers], [shouting], [tired], [excited], [serious], plus pauses [short pause] / [long pause]). Use them sparingly — most lines should have none; never decorate every line. Preserve any existing supported tags and reposition them only if it clearly improves pacing.
9.  **Speaker Grammar:** Use provided speaker metadata only for grammatical agreement and self-reference (gender/plural forms). Never change who says what.
10. **Number and Date Conversion:** Write numbers and dates as they would be spoken aloud in {target_language}.
11. **Correct Errors:** Fix grammatical errors, typos, missing translation, or awkward calques.
12. **Named Titles and Brands:** Keep film titles, character names, studio names, and release dates accurate; never translate official titles unless the input translation already did.
13. **Orthography and Diacritics:** Apply correct {target_language} orthography. For Russian, prefer 'ё' where standard usage requires it. Do not strip diacritics; use language-appropriate casing.
